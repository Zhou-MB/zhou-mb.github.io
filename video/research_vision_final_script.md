# Research vision

1. When we take the bus to work, changing road conditions can make us late.

2. A digital twin system can use sensor data to help the bus choose a route.

3. However, choosing the right route is not as simple as it seems.

4. If the digital twin system does not maintain an up-to-date view of road conditions,

5. following its instructions can still lead to costly mistakes.

6. Could we avoid this by updating every digital state immediately?

7. Unfortunately, limited bandwidth means we cannot send every update at once.

8. As updates queue up, important changes can be left waiting.

9. Some updates matter more than others for a given task.

10. Age and frequency of change offer clues, but do not capture the full picture.

11. So we need to prioritize the updates that matter to the decision.

12. Digital twin systems also need computing power to run their tasks.

13. These tasks are distributed across edge and cloud resources.

14. With limited resources and tight deadlines, deciding where and when to run them is difficult.

15. Task execution and state updates must work together to meet diverse requirements.

16. We study these challenges through scheduling models and theoretical optimization.

17. We then develop and evaluate methods for task execution and state synchronization.

18. We will also explore agentic methods that adapt scheduling as conditions change.

19. Across these settings, scheduling objectives and constraints vary widely.

20. No single scheduling method works best for every problem.

21. We also explore how AI can generate scheduling algorithms.

22. These AI-generated algorithms must meet the constraints and perform well in tests.

23. Together, these efforts help digital twin systems turn data into timely, useful decisions.

24. Our goal is to build better digital twin systems that improve everyday life.
